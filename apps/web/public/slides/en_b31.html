Electrical Resistivity (ρ):

  • Characteristic property of a material.
  • Measures how strongly a material resists current.
  • SI Unit: Ohm-metre (Ω m).
  • Low ρ = Good conductor (e.g., Copper)
  • High ρ = Insulator (e.g., Glass)
This constant, ρ (rho), is called the electrical resistivity of the material. It's a characteristic property of the material itself, telling us how strongly a specific material resists electric current, regardless of its shape or size. The SI unit of resistivity is Ohm-metre (Ω m). Materials with very low resistivity, like silver and copper, are excellent conductors, while materials with very high resistivity, like rubber and glass, are insulators.
