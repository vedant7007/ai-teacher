Example: Electric Heater

  • Initial: V = 60V, I = 4A
  • New: V = 120V
  • Since V ∝ I, if V doubles, I also doubles.
  • New Current = 8A
Let's look at an example. Imagine an electric heater that has a potential difference of 60 Volts across its terminals and draws a current of 4 Amperes from the source. If we increase the potential difference to 120 Volts, what current will the heater draw? Since V is directly proportional to I, if the potential difference doubles from 60V to 120V, the current will also double from 4A to 8A, assuming the heater's resistance remains constant.
