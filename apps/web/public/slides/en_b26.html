When you use a thicker wire, you would observe that the ammeter reading increases. This means that the thicker wire has less resistance. So, resistance is inversely proportional to the area of cross-section (R ∝ 1/A). A wider path allows more electrons to flow easily, reducing the overall opposition. This is why thick wires are often used for carrying large currents, like in household wiring. [citations: jesc111-640a680a2407:0011]
